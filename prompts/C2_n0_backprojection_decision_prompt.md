CONTEXT — READ CAREFULLY, IT CORRECTS AN EARLIER MISREADING.

Per-cell respiration is `K / N0` with `N0 = N_inoc * exp(r*delta)`, so
`log R = log K - log N_inoc - r*delta` — respiration is an explicit decreasing function of the fitted
growth rate. Median `r*delta` = 0.68 (deflation ~2x, max 36x). Removing the term raises E_R by
0.11–0.31 eV unevenly by taxon and flattens E_R across all five taxa to 0.55–0.63 eV, i.e. the entire
between-taxon ORDERING of respiratory thermal sensitivity may be generated by a term built from `r`.

TWO THINGS CONSTRAIN HOW THIS CAN BE INVESTIGATED.

(1) The cell density in a sealed vial CANNOT be measured at the window start without breaking the
    seal and destroying the closed-system oxygen measurement. The back-calculation is a considered
    design choice, not an oversight. The question is which back-calculation is best supported, never
    whether to do one. Do not recommend in-vial measurement at the window start.

(2) `delta` is NOT a biological lag. It is the time for the vial and optode to reach physico-chemical
    equilibrium after liquid is added, and the raw traces show it plainly. Pre-peak oxygen follows a
    first-order approach to equilibrium whose amplitude and time constant both scale with distance
    from ambient (Clade1 R1: +0.88 mg/L over 37 min at 22 C; +2.78 over 51 min at 34 C; +5.40 over
    70 min at 44 C). `delta` itself is ~58–82 min at EVERY temperature with no trend, and
    near-identical across taxa (Clades I–IV and para all 60–90 min; only glabrata is longer at
    100–148 min, consistent with its known signal-to-noise limit). A biological lag would be far
    shorter near 34–36 C where growth is ~3x faster than at 22 C. It is not.

Because `delta` is a DETECTION delay rather than a lag, the cells were present and growing during it,
so back-projection is directionally defensible. Two errors follow instead:

  (i)  THERMAL-RAMP ERROR. During `delta` the sample climbed from ambient to set point — ~2 C at the
       bottom of the range, ~24 C at the top. The cells were NOT growing at the final-temperature
       rate `r` for the whole interval, so `exp(r*delta)` over-corrects, worst at the extremes. This
       distorts precisely the axis the paper is about.

  (ii) FIT-WINDOW CONTAMINATION. If the transient is not settled at the oxygen peak, the early fit
       window carries residual abiotic drift on top of biological drawdown, inflating `K` — and
       inflating it more at high temperature where the transient is larger and slower. That is a
       candidate mechanism for "respiration rises monotonically to 44 C". There are NO cell-free
       wells anywhere in the design (row D and column 6 are "No Sensor"), so this can be bounded from
       the transient's own shape but not measured.

The repo also contradicts itself: `config.R:446-456` says TRUE "injects the inverted growth curve and
fit noise into respiration", while `04_trim_selector.R:247-252` says "that was wrong ... Do NOT set
N0_BACKPROJECT <- FALSE". Neither cites an analysis. C2 produces the analysis.

The same construction is in the published method (`icakin/OxygenModel`, `CUE.R:222`), so every finding
here is method-level, not Candidas-specific. Say so explicitly wherever it applies.
