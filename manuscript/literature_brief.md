# What we can show in the paper — literature-grounded positioning brief

*Synthesis of a detailed literature review across five themes (temperature–CUE, thermal-restriction/fever, C. auris clade biology, the C. haemulonii complex, and alternative respiration/AOX), mapped to what our data can defensibly claim. Every claim below is tagged **[NEW]** (genuinely novel — claim it), **[EXTENDS]** (known elsewhere, first shown here in pathogenic yeasts), or **[SUPPORTS]** (prior art — cite it, don't claim it).*

---

## 1. The headline — three things that are genuinely new

**[NEW] 1. First full-range, unimodal carbon-use-efficiency (CUE) thermal-performance curve in pathogenic yeasts.** The temperature–CUE decoupling mechanism has been shown in soil/marine bacteria and, at *two temperatures only*, in non-pathogenic marine yeasts (Heitger & Baltar 2023). No one has measured a full CUE-TPC across a thermal range in a pathogenic fungus. Our 22–44 °C curves for seven *Candida* taxa are the first.

**[NEW] 2. A carbon-use-efficiency "cost of fever" levied on the pathogen.** The entire thermal-restriction literature is either presence/absence ("can the fungus grow above a ceiling?") or host-side accounting ("what does endothermy/fever cost the mammal?"). Quantifying what a fever (37→40 °C) costs the *pathogen* in carbon-efficiency terms, resolved per taxon, is a new axis. This adds a **metabolic-efficiency dimension to the thermal-restriction hypothesis** (Bergman & Casadevall 2010): at body temperature these yeasts are not merely growing slower — they are operating *past their CUE optimum*, in a wasteful, respiration-dominated regime.

**[NEW] 3. A febrile-range (44 °C) *C. haemulonii* isolate.** Every published source reports *C. haemulonii* as unable to grow at 40–42 °C. Our thermotolerant isolate (1724) growing to 44 °C **exceeds every published maximum for the species** — the first report of febrile-range growth in *C. haemulonii*. (Caveat: make its ITS/D1-D2 identification airtight in the paper, because a reviewer will suspect *C. auris* misidentification.)

---

## 2. The framing arc (Introduction → Discussion)

The cleanest logical spine, with the citations that carry each beat:

1. **Endothermy/fever is an antifungal barrier.** Every 1 °C over 30–40 °C excludes ~6% of fungal species (Robert & Casadevall 2009, n = 4,802 strains); modelled optimum body temperature 36.7 °C (Bergman & Casadevall 2010). *(Note: the 6%/°C figure is one 2009 dataset restated in 2010 & 2016 — cite as one source, not three.)*
2. **But that barrier is defined by growth/no-growth — it is blind to the cost paid by taxa that survive above their optimum.** ← our wedge.
3. **Climate change may select thermotolerant fungal pathogens; *C. auris* is the poster child** (Casadevall, Kontoyiannis & Robert 2019). Balance with the skeptics (de Crécy et al. 2024; the environmental-*C. auris* rebuttal, mBio 2021).
4. **CUE decouples from temperature because growth and respiration have different activation energies** — established in bacteria (Smith et al. 2021; Pold et al. 2020) and general thermal theory (Dell et al. 2011), but never mapped in pathogenic fungi.
5. **We measure it, and find every CUE optimum sits below body temperature, with a taxon-specific fever cost that tracks known thermal/clinical biology.**

## 3. Result-by-result: what we can claim

### 3a. Growth is ~2× more temperature-sensitive than respiration → CUE turns over
- **[EXTENDS]** The mechanism (E_growth > E_resp; growth TPC peaks below respiration's; this drives CUE down above the growth optimum) is **canonical in bacteria** — Smith et al. 2021 (*Ecol Lett*) found E_μ ≈ 0.84 vs E_R ≈ 0.66 eV (~1.3×) across 29 isolates + >400 curves; Pold et al. 2020 (*mBio*). Ours (~2×) is the same direction, larger magnitude, first in yeasts. **Claim: "we extend a bacterial/soil mechanism to pathogenic yeasts and quantify a larger sensitivity differential."** Do NOT claim you discovered the mechanism.
- **[SUPPORTS]** Frame your unimodal CUE curve as *reconciling* the contested "does CUE fall with warming?" literature: it falls, is flat, or rises depending on where the sampled range sits relative to the growth optimum (Allison 2010; Frey 2013; Li 2019 for decline; Hagerty 2014; Smith 2021 for flat/rising). This is a strong, defensible synthesis move.

### 3b. Every CUE optimum lies below body temperature (~29–34 °C < 37 °C)
- **[NEW/EXTENDS]** The "efficiency optimum sits below the growth optimum" idea is implied by Smith et al. 2021 and shown bioenergetically in *S. cerevisiae* (Zakhartsev et al. 2015). Locating it below *host body temperature* across seven pathogenic taxa is new. **Claim it as the metabolic-efficiency extension of Bergman & Casadevall (2010).**

### 3c. The fever cost gradient (clade IV cheapest → clade II most expensive)
- **[SUPPORTS, with a hard caveat]** Your fever-cost ranking is **directionally concordant** with published *C. auris* clade virulence: murine bloodstream mortality IV 96% > I 80% > III 45% ≈ II 44% (Forgács et al. 2020); clade IV most virulent in silkworm (Kurakado et al. 2023); clade II the metabolically/thermally weakest lineage (Welsh 2019; Byun 2023 — reduced 42 °C thermotolerance; Brandt 2023 — most metabolically restricted).
- **THE TRAP (state this plainly):** with n = 4 phylogenetically non-independent clades, a *perfect* rank match gives p ≈ 0.08 — significance is mathematically unreachable, and phylogenetic-comparative correction is unfittable at 4 tips. **Present it as a side-by-side concordance table, never as a correlation coefficient or p-value, and never causally.** Human-mortality clade differences are themselves not significant (Santana et al. 2024).

### 3d. C. haemulonii & C. duobushaemulonii are cool-adapted / fever-restricted
- **[SUPPORTS]** Strongly backed. *C. haemulonii*: optimal ~30 °C, poor/absent at 37 °C, no growth at 40–42 °C (Ben-Ami et al. 2017; Gómez-Gaviria et al. 2023). Marine origin — first isolated from Atlantic seawater/fish gut (van Uden & Kolipinski 1962; Ahearn 2023) — gives a clean ecological rationale for cool adaptation. Clinical tropism is cutaneous/nail/catheter-associated, less invasive than *C. auris* (Ben-Ami 2017; Santos 2022). **Claim: our TPCs provide a physiological correlate for the complex's restriction to cooler body sites and lower invasive virulence.**
- **HANDLE CAREFULLY:** the type description scores *C. duobushaemulonii* as *growth-positive at 37 °C* (Cendejas-Bueno 2012; Gade 2020: "most grow well at body temperature"). This is **not** a contradiction — a binary "grows at 37 °C = yes" ≠ an *optimum* at 37 °C. Frame your TPC as *refining* the binary record (37 °C growth, where present, is well below optimal). Do **not** claim duo "cannot grow at 37 °C."
- **[SUPPORTS]** Strain-level thermal variation in the complex is documented ("poor or absent" at 37 °C; "most strains cannot grow at 37 °C" — Ben-Ami 2017; Gade 2020), which licenses your one-thermotolerant/two-sensitive isolate split as a quantification of known variation — with 1724's 44 °C growth as the novel extreme (§1.3).

### 3e. C. parapsilosis is 3× more respiratory-expensive — the AOX interpretation
- **[SUPPORTS, as interpretation not proof]** *C. parapsilosis* is a textbook organism for a constitutive, well-developed **parallel non-phosphorylating respiratory branch** (Guérin & Camougrand 1986; Camougrand & Guérin 1994 — "parallel working of the two chains"; Milani et al. 2001 — constitutive AOX + parallel chain, ADP/O appraisal). Such branches consume O₂ at reduced ADP/O → more O₂ per ATP → higher effective maintenance respiration (Duvenage et al. 2019 + basic bioenergetics). The *haemulonii* clade is, independently, fermentation-leaning/low-respiring (Huang et al. 2024, by O₂-consumption + AOX inhibitors) — consistent with your low-respiration cluster.
- **THE HONESTY PIVOT (put this in the paper):** AOX is **near-ubiquitous** in respiratory *Candida* (Veiga 2003; present & functional in *C. auris*, Copsey 2021; inhibitor-responsive in *haemulonii*, Huang 2024). So the 3× is **not** a gene presence/absence difference — it is **flux/regulation**, which **genomics and standard FBA cannot resolve** (plain FBA sets wasteful AOX flux to ~0). Claim the interpretation as *consistent with* greater alternative-pathway flux; state that confirming it needs SHAM/cyanide inhibitor partitioning or AOX transcript data — experiments not done here. This turns the caveat into a strength and pre-empts the "why not a GEM?" question.
- **Clinical hook:** AOX is a virulence factor and antifungal target across *Candida* incl. *C. auris* (Liu et al. 2023; Copsey et al. 2021; Duvenage et al. 2019) — one sentence links your metabolic finding to therapy.

## 4. Skeptical flags to pre-empt (a reviewer will hit these)

1. **"CUE declines with warming" is contested** (Hagerty 2014 found it constant; Smith 2021 mostly rising in-range). Use the unimodal-curve reconciliation, don't add another "decline" vote.
2. **Absolute CUE is an operational, O₂-derived estimate** — different from ¹³C/¹⁸O soil CUE; state your definition; lead on scale-free quantities (optima, activation energies, ratios).
3. **The clade fever-cost↔virulence link is concordance, not correlation** (n=4, phylogenetic non-independence) — table, not p-value.
4. **The para AOX story is interpretation, not measurement** — hedge as above.
5. **The 44 °C haemulonii isolate** — lock down its identification.
6. **AOX heat-induction is a hypothesis** — it's shown in other fungi (Zhang 2021, *Pleurotus*), not yet in *Candida*; don't state it as fact.

## 5. Does any of this become a figure/table? (honest)

- **A concordance table** (clade × fever cost × virulence × drug resistance × thermotolerance) is legitimate and strong — a table, not a plot, sidesteps the n=4 statistics problem. Good candidate for a main-text or supplementary **table**.
- The para AOX story is a **Discussion paragraph**, not a figure (flux, not measured here).
- The cool-adapted haemulonii physiology and the 44 °C isolate are already covered by your existing figures/supplement.
- **Net:** the literature adds *interpretive depth and a table*, not a new results figure — consistent with the earlier conclusion that this is a two-figure study with a deep, well-cited Discussion.
